## Algorithm ranking signals (as of the last pull)

- Ranking weighs **watch-time-to-length ratio + replay rate** over raw
  3-second view counts — a fully-watched short Reel outranks a long one
  watched briefly.
- **DM shares (sends)** were the most heavily-weighted signal for
  reaching non-follower audiences — content has to be worth forwarding,
  not just watchable.
- >70% 3-second retention still gates wider initial distribution.
- ~40% of mobile traffic watches muted — on-screen text is load-bearing,
  not decorative.

## Six proven hook categories (ranked by measured 3-second retention at
## time of research)

1. Pattern Interrupt — 72-84%
2. Curiosity Gap — 65-78%
3. Direct Question — 58-72%
4. Bold Claim — 55-70%
5. Problem Hook — 50-65%
6. Social Proof Hook — 45-60%

Named-format results from a separate source: Specific Outcome hooks
("I went from X to Y in 30 days") ~45% retention; POV Realism hooks
("POV: you finally figured out...") ~42%, cited as the strongest
Reels-native opener format; Unpopular Opinion/Contrarian ~38%.

## The 3-layer hook formula

Fire all three in the first **1.0-1.5 seconds**, with the payoff promise
landing by **3.0 seconds**:
1. A pattern-interrupt **visual** (something the eye doesn't expect).
2. An emotional-trigger **verbal/character** line — not narration
   preamble ("Hey guys, welcome back..." is the single most-cited
   retention killer).
3. A curiosity-gap **text overlay**, for the muted-viewer share.

## Character/mascot findings

Strong, consistent character branding measurably lifts engagement.
The 2026 bar for a "real" brand character: it has to work as a still
image, a sticker, a tiny UI accent, *and* a short animated beat — not
just a one-off animation. Recurring, recognizable characters compound
audience familiarity across episodes more than any single episode's
polish does.

## Countervailing signal — don't over-polish

Low-production, one-take, unedited-feeling content is *also* winning big
right now. Read this as: the hook and the character's voice need to feel
authentic and specific, not that animated content needs to look rough on
purpose. A rough-edged, expressive 2-second character beat beats a smooth
but inert one — this is consistent with, not opposed to, "real character
animation over PPT-style motion graphics."

## Applying this per account/brand

Match hook category to brand voice rather than defaulting to whichever
category tests highest in the abstract — a Pattern Interrupt/POV hook
suits a fast, punchy, comedic account; a calm, editorial-voice brand may
be better served by a Problem Hook or Bold Claim delivered at its own
pace than by importing a punchier account's hook style wholesale. See
`references/animation-principles.md` and the main `SKILL.md`'s
per-account tone-differentiation step — hook category and edit pacing
should be decided together, not the hook chosen generically and the
pacing borrowed from whatever account was built first.
